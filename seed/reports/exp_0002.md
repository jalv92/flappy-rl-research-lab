# Experiment 2: H007 — Oversampling the last steps before a crash teaches obstacle avoidance sooner

- Stage: **screening** (exploratory) · profile `full_research` · code `2ae67e4` · variant source `6052636ba07b`
- Decision: **inconclusive**
- Conclusion: no significant difference. auc: diff -0.0493 (95% CI -0.187..+0.0787, P(improve)=0.44); final_mean: diff +13.9 (95% CI -94.6..+116, P(improve)=0.66)

## Hypothesis
- Area: experience_replay
- Motivation: The decisions that matter are those in the ~0.5 s before a collision; they are identifiable without TD errors.
- Modification: Half of each batch is sampled from transitions within 15 steps before a termination.
- Expected benefit: Higher AUC; cheaper than PER (no priority maintenance).
- Failure modes considered: Uncorrected sampling bias over-weights failure states and may distort Q-values elsewhere.
- Prior work: Related: PER (Schaul et al. 2016); episodic backward update (Lee et al. 2019).
- Novelty status: unverified
- Baseline: xdqn control: identical hyperparameters, step budget, seeds and evaluation schedule
- Criteria: Primary: validation AUC (sample efficiency) and final-policy validation mean, per training seed, candidate vs control. Promising only if a 95% bootstrap CI of the difference excludes 0 in the favourable direction and no primary metric is significantly worse. Secondary: degradation count, seed CV, wall-clock ratio.

## Setup
- Variant `crash_focus` vs `control`, 150000 env steps per run, training seeds [0, 1, 2, 3, 4]
- Validation: 16 fixed episodes every 10000 steps; final policy on 50 episodes
- Runs: {'control': [11, 12, 13, 14, 15], 'candidate': [23, 24, 25, 26, 27]}

## Limitations
- Bootstrap CIs over a handful of seeds are coarse; exploratory stages are not evidence.
- CPU training is not bit-reproducible across machines/BLAS builds; seeds control everything else.
- The score cap (200) saturates strong agents; differences above it are invisible.
