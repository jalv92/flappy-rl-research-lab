## Hypothesis
- Area: loss_and_update_rules
- Motivation: The control's validation curves reach the score cap and then fall back to near zero. Max-operator overestimation is a classic driver of such Q-value blow-ups.
- Modification: Select the bootstrap action with the online network and evaluate it with the target network.
- Expected benefit: Fewer catastrophic degradations and a higher final-policy mean at equal steps.
- Failure modes considered: With only two actions and sparse rewards the overestimation bias may be small; no effect.
- Prior work: van Hasselt, Guez & Silver 2016 (Double DQN).
- Novelty status: known
- Baseline: xdqn control: identical hyperparameters, step budget, seeds and evaluation schedule
- Criteria: Primary: validation AUC (sample efficiency) and final-policy validation mean, per training seed, candidate vs control. Promising only if a 95% bootstrap CI of the difference excludes 0 in the favourable direction and no primary metric is significantly worse. Secondary: degradation count, seed CV, wall-clock ratio.

## Setup
- Variant `double` vs `control`, 150000 env steps per run, training seeds [0, 1, 2, 3, 4]
- Validation: 16 fixed episodes every 10000 steps; final policy on 50 episodes
- Runs: {'control': [11, 12, 13, 14, 15], 'candidate': [29, 30, 31, 32, 33]}
- Reused identical control runs: [11, 12, 13, 14, 15]

## Limitations
- Bootstrap CIs over a handful of seeds are coarse; exploratory stages are not evidence.
- CPU training is not bit-reproducible across machines/BLAS builds; seeds control everything else.
- The score cap (200) saturates strong agents; differences above it are invisible.
